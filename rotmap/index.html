How routing works Click hexes to add waypoints. Drag a waypoint to move it; the route updates while you drag. Right-click a hex for route actions, including adding or removing a waypoint and starting a new route. Remove last is the touch-friendly way to remove the final waypoint. Ctrl+Z undoes.

Click a route to select it, click its swatch to recolour it, and double-click its row to open the step table. The ⋯ menu renames, duplicates, clears, copies, or pastes a route. A route starting under a token uses that token's colour.

In the step table, right-click a step to start or end a forced march, or to split the route there.

Spread

How the isochrone works Click the map to move the selected origin. Drag an origin to update its reach live. Outside this panel, Shift+click moves it. Right-click a hex to add, move, or remove an origin. Each origin has its own colour and column settings.

With several origins, each subhex belongs to the one that reaches it first. Hover to see the runner-up and the time difference. Use an origin's swatch, name, or ⋯ menu to edit it.

Coasts and major rivers can split a hex into separate subhexes. Only reachable subhexes are shaded. Open water is shown only if the column has a fleet or may secure one.

Messages and rumours travel in a straight line, so column settings do not apply.

Place the origin at the siege, battle, or landing. Shading shows where a relief force could start and still arrive within the time limit.

How a relief army works The selected subhex is the destination. News travels outward in a straight line; the relief force then marches back using the selected column and conditions. The shaded time is the sum of both legs. Hover a subhex to see the split.

Each leg is rounded up to a whole day before they are added. Half a day for the news and half a day for the march therefore costs two days.

The return march is calculated toward the origin, so destination terrain and one-way costs are charged correctly.

How long you actually have

For a siege, add the expected siege length to the time needed to secure the stronghold after it falls: 1 IRL day for a fortress, 1d6 for a town, or 2d6 for a city.

For a battle, set the time to however long you expect the line to hold. The default of 4 days is only a starting estimate.

Board

Token positions are saved in this browser. Use Export to keep a backup or share them. Legion colour updates recolour matching tokens without moving them; Ctrl+Z restores the previous colours. Starting positions resets all tokens to the shipped board.

How tokens work Right-click a hex and choose Mark as, or press Place token and tap a hex. Legion tokens use their legion's Warlords colour; other tokens use a separate palette.

Drag a token to move it. While this panel is open, right-click a token to rename, recolour, split, or delete it. On touchscreens, hold the token for this menu. Ctrl+Z undoes token, route, and drawing changes in the order they were made.

Autosaved locally.

Drawing changes are saved in this browser. Use Export JSON to back them up or share them.

Using the tools

Placing points Click to add snapped points. A new point may be at most one hex from the last. Enter or double-click finishes; Esc cancels; Ctrl+Z undoes. Hold Alt for free placement or E to snap along the nearest edge.
Coast A coast stays within the first hex you click. Add its points, then click the sea side. Click the starting point after at least three points to close a loop, such as a small island. Hold E to snap anywhere along an edge. Shift+click an existing coast to change which side is sea.
Map painting Choose Borders or Warlords, then click to paint a subhex or drag to paint several. Selecting the active colour again removes your override. Nobody clears a claim from the source scan. Painted changes are included in the features JSON.

Custom colour… adds a colour to the palette. Pick from map samples the next subhex. Warlords also includes colours assigned to tokens. Double-click a swatch to pan to the largest area using that colour.

Right-click the map to paint or clear one subhex, paint a whole commandery, undo, or clear all manual paint on the current layer. Commandery painting covers land only.

Double-click a colour name to rename it. Colours with the same name are treated as one polity for map labels, while remaining separate paint colours. Clear a custom name to use the built-in legend again.

The A button on the Borders and Warlords layer rows shows realm names on the map. The local Realm labels panel controls their fitting, font, and collision settings.

Stronghold Click to place or restore a stronghold. Shift+click toggles port status; ports have a blue ring. Right-click the hex to set it as ordinary, fortress, or major. Strongholds beside the sea or a major river are ports by default.
Label Click a hex to name or rename it. Clear the text to restore the datasheet name. Commandery names follow their highest-ranked settlement.

The A button on the Strongholds layer toggles names without hiding the markers. Stronghold names are drawn below realm names.

Erase Click a node to remove it, click a segment to cut it, or Shift+click to remove the whole line. Drag to erase features and strongholds. Datasheet strongholds stay hidden until restored or undone.

Adjust how realm names are fitted. Turn on Borders or Warlords and its A label switch to preview changes.
