How routing works Click hexes to add waypoints to the active route. Right-click a hex to start a fresh route there, or to remove the last waypoint from this one; right-click a hex the route stops in to remove that waypoint, wherever it sits in the route. Remove last does the same for touchscreens, where there is no right button. Ctrl+Z undoes. Click a route's swatch to recolour it, double-click its name to rename. A route that sets out from a hex a token is standing on takes that token's colour, so a legion's march is drawn in the legion's own colour. In the readout, right-click a step to force the march from there, end the push, or split the route in two at that hex — each plants a waypoint, since the pace is solved leg by leg.

How tokens work Right-click a hex and choose Mark as to drop a counter on it — I to XIV, or text of your own. After that: click it to cycle its colour, drag it to another hex, right-click it to rename, recolour, split off a detachment (V → V'a → V'b) or remove. The rows above answer the right button the same way. Ctrl+Z takes back a placement, a move, a colour or a deletion — one stack shared with the routes and the drawing, so it always undoes whatever you did last. On a touchscreen, press Place token and then tap a hex; hold a token for its menu.

Using the tools

Placing points Click to add snapped points — corners, edge midpoints, centres, sub-points, and the vertices of features you have already drawn, so new lines connect precisely — at most one hex ahead per click, so nothing gets skipped. Enter or double-click finishes, Esc cancels, Ctrl+Z undoes. Hold Alt for free placement, or E to snap anywhere along the nearest hex edge.
Coast Locked to a single hex: the first click picks the hex, and points snap only to that hex's corners, edges, centre and sub-points (hold E to snap anywhere along an edge). Points also snap back onto the line you are drawing — once you have three, the starting node turns into a teal ring, and clicking it closes the loop and finishes the line. That is how you draw an island smaller than a hex. Then click the side that is sea. Coast lines act as barriers, so two coasts can enclose an inner sea; works on land and sea hexes alike. Shift+click a coast later to re-pick its sea side.
Erase Click a node to remove just it, click between nodes to cut that segment (which splits the line), Shift+click to remove a whole line, or drag across the map to wipe features and strongholds as you go. Clicking a stronghold marker removes it — including datasheet strongholds, which are hidden persistently; Ctrl+Z or the Stronghold tool brings them back.
Stronghold and Label Stronghold: click to place or restore the marker within its hex; Shift+click toggles coastal/port — a blue ring means a port, where embarking and disembarking are possible. Sea- and river-side strongholds are ports by default, and Shift+click overrides that either way for the exceptions. Label: click any hex to name or rename it, which works on datasheet strongholds too; clear the text to fall back to the datasheet name.
